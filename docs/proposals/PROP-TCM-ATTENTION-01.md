Each would need independent investigation and source-specific clinical examination.

## 3. Topology: acupoints are NOT a continuous Qi conduit

| Code | Chinese | Location / classification | Permitted symbolic role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **EX-HN3** | 印堂 Yintang | Glabella, between eyebrows; **EXTRA point, outside WHO 361 standard point inventory** | Optional gentle self-acupressure landmark and starting focus region |
| **CV6** | 氣海 Qihai | Anterior midline, 1.5 proportional body-cun below navel | *Nearby* lower-abdomen landmark, not identical to the entire lower dantian |
| **CV5** | 石門 Shimen | Anterior midline, 2 cun below navel; `丹田` is a classical alias in this edition | Term ambiguity witness (do NOT press or needle) |
| **CV4** | 關元 Guanyuan | Anterior midline, 3 cun below navel | Alternative lower-abdomen landmark |
| **Lower dantian** | 下丹田 | Practice-defined lower-abdominal region, often described beyond one surface point; school dependent | **Conceptual attention destination** |

**Critical correction to 361-point schema:** `EX-HN3` must be classified as an **extra point**, not inserted as the 362nd standard point of the 14-channel taxonomy. Retain standard-361 cardinality unchanged.

**No automatic meridian-transport edge:** EX-HN3 and CV4/CV5/CV6 are distinct atlas nodes. A mental attention sequence between them is a `subjective_attention_sequence` edge, not a nerve/vascular/qi conduit. BIRCA must never recode it as measured conductivity or blocked flow.

For location nomenclature (not efficacy): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3929431/ and classical Dacheng above.

## 4. Attention-only practice annotation: optional, gentle, non-forcing

This is a **modern, conservatively assembled self-relaxation sequence** drawing on source-separated traditions, *not an ancient unified prescription*. It is not for diagnosing or "clearing blocked physical Qi."

1. **Posture / groundedness (30–60 s):** sit comfortably, keep eyes softly closed or open, feet supported; relax brow, jaw, shoulders; breathe in an unforced rhythm.
2. **EX-HN3 (30–60 s, optionally up to 2 min):** with clean finger/thumb, touch midpoint between eyebrows on forehead (NOT bridge of nose or eyeballs), make small light circles. Do not press through pain; **skip if irritated, infected, injured or inflamed skin**.
3. **Release rather than push (30–60 s):** stop pressing; note tension in forehead, temples, jaw and upper chest, then soften those muscles; no pressing throat/sternum or attempting manual pressure along any "meridian".
4. **Attentional descent (1–3 min):** let attention rest successively on comfortable natural breathing, chest movement, upper abdomen, then **lower abdomen below navel**. Optionally rest an open palm lightly on the lower abdomen; do not press CV4/CV5/CV6 or force belly protrusion.
5. **Return / check (30 s):** sense feet/contact with seat, open eyes; record brow tension and irritability at start/end on a user-defined 0–10 scale if desired. Lower score would show a *subjective association for that session only*, not "Qi cleared".

Never hold the breath, strain, hyperventilate, push pressure into the head/eyes, try breath-driven microcosmic-orbit circulation or compel sensations to move. Stop if headache, dizziness, chest pain, escalating distress, visual disturbance, or uncomfortable breath occurs. Persistent/new/worsening head pressure, numbness, severe headache or vision change warrants medical evaluation rather than Qi interpretation.

**Modern patient-education source (self-care method, *not a PubMed efficacy result*):** Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, Yintang acupressure: https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/patient-education/acupressure-stress-and-anxiety . Institutional safety/educational instructions do **not** override BIRCA's PubMed-only biomedical evidence gate.
